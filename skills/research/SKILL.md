# Research

Answer the research question with traceable evidence and source material that can support later work.

## Frame the question

Read the existing context and research first. Establish the questions, intended use, user-confirmed constraints, required depth, and relevant dates or versions. Clarify only missing information that would materially change the investigation. A bounded factual question can be answered directly from the relevant source.

Choose the delivery mode before collecting material. Default to durable delivery in the existing Task or another authorized location, following the host's conventions. For conversation-only delivery, return findings, citations, and evidence gaps in the conversation. File writes, saved-source requirements, and cross-session resumption below apply only to durable delivery. If the write location needs confirmation, continue authorized reading and analysis, then identify what needs saving.

Proceed when each question has a clear subject and evidence need, and the delivery mode is known.

## Gather evidence

For an open topic, map the important dimensions and competing perspectives before choosing where to dig deeper. For a bounded question, go directly to the relevant records, documentation, data, or implementation. Reuse existing material after checking its relevance and currency. Let unanswered questions determine search breadth and source count.

Prefer primary sources responsible for the facts under investigation. Follow citations and secondary accounts back to the original evidence. Read the material supporting important claims in context; search snippets and summaries help locate that material. Record which pages, sections, or versions were actually examined.

### Preserve sources

During durable delivery, save acquired material alongside its provenance as collection proceeds:

- Prefer a readable copy of the original content. Retain the original format when it matters to interpretation or verification. Label excerpts and summaries as such.
- Record the title, original URL or file path, available author and publication date, access date, and relevant version, commit, page, or section. Preserve attribution and link the saved copy back to its origin.
- Use document-relative links to saved material. Keep evidence needed for the findings in durable storage. Follow host rules for large downloads and working data, and identify sources whose only local copy may be cleared.
- For inaccessible or partially retrieved sources, retain the information actually obtained, the access limitation, and its reason. Treat unavailable content as a gap in the evidence.

Use the existing research layout. A small investigation can keep its findings and source records in one Markdown document. Preserve original source material separately when the findings document would otherwise replace it with a summary.

Collection is ready for assessment when every key question has relevant material or a specific access gap. A gap that could change the answer directs further investigation within the agreed scope.

## Check claims

Distinguish source statements, verified facts, inferences, judgments, and unresolved questions. Connect each key finding to the evidence that supports it. Several accounts repeating one original claim form one evidence chain.

A primary source can establish its own recorded actions or claims. Evaluate evidence for effects, comparisons, and disputed claims on its merits, including relevant independent evidence. Check conflicting accounts for differences in dates, versions, definitions, samples, and scope. Preserve a disagreement when the available evidence leaves it unresolved, and state what would distinguish the competing explanations.

Use an available thinking-method skill when a specific causal, modeling, or reasoning gap needs it. Match confidence and scope to the evidence obtained.

Proceed when every key finding has a traceable basis and applicable conditions, or is explicitly unresolved.

## Deliver findings

Organize the answer around the agreed questions. Cite the evidence for key conclusions and make the distinction between observations, inferences, and recommendations clear. Explain how unresolved gaps affect the answer.

For durable delivery, maintain a research document containing the questions and scope, supported findings, source locations, unresolved issues, and evidence-based next steps where needed. Another session should be able to locate the original material and distinguish completed checks from remaining work. Keep Task identity and lifecycle under the host's existing management tools.

Finish when each agreed question has a supported answer or an identified evidence gap with its impact and conditions for continuing. Further collection needs a concrete gap to address.

## Delegate collection

Keep responsibility for the questions, evidence assessment, and synthesis in the main session. When independently bounded collection work benefits from delegation, follow the host's collaboration rules. Give each assignment its question, source boundaries, delivery mode, authorized destination where needed, and return requirements. Check returned material for overlapping sources, actual retrieval, and claims that exceed the evidence before using it.
