# First-principle

Reconsider an approach from grounded facts, explicit goals, and necessary constraints when inherited assumptions are in doubt.

## Method

1. State the actual goal and the part of the existing approach being questioned.
2. Separate supported facts, provisional assumptions, conventions, chosen goals, and constraints. Identify which constraints are fixed and which could change.
3. Check the premises that can change the conclusion. A simple-sounding premise still needs evidence or an explicit agreement.
4. Derive feasible approaches from the retained premises. Reuse established knowledge where it applies, and preserve the user's goals and authorized scope.
5. Compare the approaches on the costs and consequences relevant to the decision. Identify a proportionate observation or experiment for any pivotal uncertainty; perform it only within the current permission.

## Result

Show the premises that matter, the assumption being revised, and the resulting approach or tradeoff. Keep chosen values and goals explicit. Facts alone do not determine what the user should value.

A novel solution must still fit the actual environment. Include practical constraints that materially affect whether it works.

## Completion

Finish when the disputed assumption has been examined deeply enough to support the current decision. Further reduction needs a specific unresolved question.
