# Why

Explain why an outcome, choice, or persistent arrangement arose using evidence appropriate to that question.

## Method

1. Identify whether the question concerns causes, a person's decision, persistence over time, or present-day suitability.
2. Reconstruct the relevant circumstances. For a decision, establish the goals, information, constraints, and feasible alternatives available at the time.
3. Collect evidence that can distinguish plausible explanations. Separate recorded intent, observed effects, and your own hypotheses.
4. Compare the explanations against supporting and conflicting evidence. Consider multiple contributing causes when the facts call for them.
5. Check whether removing a proposed cause would plausibly change the outcome. Treat this counterfactual as a question to investigate.

## Result

Give the best-supported explanation, its evidence, and any uncertainty that changes the interpretation. For decisions, explain the actual tradeoff when the record supports one. Keep historical reasons separate from an assessment of today's options.

Attribute motives and beliefs to an identified source. State an unrecorded rationale as unknown, and label alternative motives as hypotheses. Evaluate the observed effects separately. A recorded reason may describe one contributing factor among several.

## Completion

Finish when the requested reason is supported at an appropriate level of confidence, or when the remaining possibilities and the evidence needed to distinguish them are clear.
