# Analogy

Use a familiar case to understand a new one by mapping the relationships that matter to the current question.

## Method

1. Identify what needs explaining or solving in the target. Recall or find a source case with relevant goals, constraints, or causal relationships.
2. Establish what is known about each case. Correct a mistaken source account before transferring it.
3. Map elements by their roles and relationships. Check that the correspondences remain consistent across the part of the structure being used.
4. State the candidate insight or inference this mapping suggests for the target.
5. Check the inference against target facts. Identify differences that could break the transfer and the conditions under which it remains useful.

## Result

Explain the shared relationship, what it helps infer, and where the comparison stops applying. A short example can carry the mapping. Use an explicit correspondence list when the structure is harder to follow.

Shared words or appearances can help retrieve a source. Relational correspondence supports the useful inference. Treat the inferred conclusion as a hypothesis until the target evidence supports it.

## Completion

Finish when the analogy has clarified the question or produced a useful, checkable inference with its boundary. If the proposed mapping fails, explain the decisive mismatch and continue with a method suited to the target.
