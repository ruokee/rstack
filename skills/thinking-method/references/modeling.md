# Modeling

Represent the concepts and relationships needed to explain, predict, or decide something.

## Method

1. State what the model must help answer. Choose its scope and level of detail accordingly.
2. Identify the relevant concepts and give overloaded terms a clear meaning in this context. Keep distinct concepts separate even when their everyday names overlap.
3. Describe relationships, states, transitions, quantities, or constraints as the question requires. Preserve distinctions that change the outcome.
4. Test the model with concrete ordinary and boundary cases. Label invented cases as illustrative and compare the model with actual evidence where available.
5. Resolve contradictions or mark the specific assumption that remains open. In a software setting, compare the proposed domain account with the behavior of the implementation.

## Result

Choose the smallest useful representation: definitions, a relationship description, a diagram, a state model, or equations. State what the model omits and when an omission matters.

Use an existing glossary or decision record when maintaining such material is authorized and the content belongs there. Let the requested purpose determine the artifact. A modeling conversation can be completed in the response itself.

## Completion

Finish when the model answers the intended question, handles the relevant examples, and makes consequential ambiguities or assumptions explicit.
