# Distill subdomains

A subdomain groups knowledge and activities in the problem space. Distillation makes the knowledge worth special modeling effort visible. Classify it relative to the current organization's purpose and strategy.

## Locate the investment

A core domain contains knowledge that differentiates the organization and deserves deliberate mastery. Supporting knowledge provides capabilities specific to this business. Generic knowledge has mature, broadly reusable approaches that can satisfy the need. These classifications can change as goals and available solutions change.

Business necessity, implementation difficulty, transaction volume, and competitive differentiation are separate facts. A critical settlement capability can be well served by a standard financial solution. Proprietary pricing rules may instead be the knowledge that makes the product valuable.

Constructed case: pricing differentiates the organization, settlement is required for survival, and an existing financial package already meets settlement requirements. Invest in understanding and expressing the pricing rules. Assess reuse or purchase for the standard settlement capability, including integration, operation, and supplier costs. Examine any business-specific settlement gap before classifying it as generic.

Explain who uses each part, the decisions it supports, the knowledge it embodies, and why deeper modeling is worth its cost. Procurement and staffing choices follow actual authority and organizational conditions.

## Make the core visible

A domain vision statement briefly explains the distinctive knowledge and value that justify the effort. Use concrete business outcomes and the interests it serves. Revise the statement when new understanding changes the priority. Length follows the explanatory need.

A highlighted core identifies the model elements carrying that knowledge. A small model explanation or marks in existing materials can be sufficient. Readers should be able to find the key relationships and recognize when a change affects them. Highlighting supplies orientation; it leaves implementation coupling to be addressed where necessary.

## Separate distracting knowledge

Generic subdomains can be isolated or reused so specialized business rules remain clear. Their necessary responsibilities remain part of the overall solution. Match the integration contract to the actual business meaning instead of embedding proprietary assumptions into a supposedly general facility.

Cohesive mechanisms isolate complicated algorithms behind an intention-revealing operation. For example, a scheduling model can express constraints and delegate search to an algorithmic mechanism. The model retains the meaning of a feasible or preferred result. The mechanism owns the details of finding it. An extra framework is unnecessary when a simple calculation already communicates the rule.

A segregated core separates core concepts from supporting responsibilities that obscure them. Refine the model and code where the current coupling prevents understanding or change. Separation can add interfaces and coordination, so make the benefit visible in real scenarios.

An abstract core extracts stable, differentiating relationships that explain important interactions among concrete parts. Keep those relationships grounded in actual models. Premature abstraction can hide meaningful differences and make later insight harder to express.

## Relate the models

A subdomain map explains problem-space knowledge. A context map explains the models that implement or interpret it. An existing context may mix several subdomains. One subdomain may have separate models for sales, planning, or accounting purposes. Describe their mapping and implications instead of assuming one-to-one boundaries.

A focused, already understandable model can remain as it is. Adopt distillation techniques for an actual problem of priority, understanding, or coupling. Their result is a clear modeling investment and a model whose important knowledge can be found.
