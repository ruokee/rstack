# Evolution

Change a system while accounting for the results and obligations that must survive. A new goal, an actual bottleneck, supplier withdrawal, end of support, or cost change can all justify revisiting its structure.

## Define the replacement

Name the reason for change, preserved outcomes, affected participants, replaceable capability, and existing obligations. Check actual records and contracts for work the old arrangement quietly performs. A replacement must account for that work or explicitly change the obligation with the authorized owner.

Choose between complete cutover and temporary coexistence. Complete cutover can be simpler when all consumers can change together, in-flight work can be resolved, and the interruption is acceptable. Coexistence can reduce the size of each change while adding duplicate operation, synchronization, and coordination. Their costs follow actual continuity and compatibility needs.

## Assign each transition stage

For every stage, state who accepts new work, holds authoritative facts or materials, allocates resources, confirms completion, and handles exceptions. Define how work already in progress reaches completion and how the next participant accepts responsibility.

Constructed service example: a new repair provider begins accepting appointments on a named date. The old provider finishes its accepted jobs, with a list of cases and parts held. The customer-contact role remains explicit. Any transferred case needs an acceptance record, a current promise, and parts custody confirmation. Both providers appearing on a list leaves those duties unresolved.

State the entry condition, intended change, completion evidence, and recovery condition for a consequential stage. A rehearsal or limited rollout is useful when it resolves a real uncertainty. Its need follows the change's risk and available evidence.

## Preserve a recovery path

Recovery starts from the actual current state. Returning to the old arrangement requires it to possess current facts, required resources, valid contracts, and authority to resume. New work accepted only by the replacement can make a direct return incomplete. Define transfer back, reconciliation, or completion on the new side where needed.

Identify irreversible effects such as consumed materials, completed payments, or promises already given to customers. They need business-valid compensation or a recovery arrangement. A routing switch alone leaves them intact.

## Retire responsibility

Retire the old capability after remaining consumers and obligations have been accounted for, custody has transferred, and required records remain accessible. End obsolete schedules, access, supply commitments, and operating work according to the actual environment. Preserve the record needed to explain the decision and recover historical facts.

Record what changed, why the new allocation meets the goal, its accepted operating cost, and conditions for revisiting. Software mechanisms for compatibility, concurrent backfill, shadow execution, and authority cutover are in [Migration](../software/migration.md).
