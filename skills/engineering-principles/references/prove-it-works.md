# Prove it works

The evidence needed for completion follows the claim being made. Identify the artifact and the behavior promised, then seek an observation that could expose a plausible failure of that promise.

For a file-transfer feature, a local success message establishes that the sender reported success. Inspecting the destination file under the exercised conditions can establish receipt and content. For an editable guide, a successful formatting check establishes formatting under its configuration. A reader finding and carrying out the instruction supplies evidence about usability. Choose the observation at the boundary the claim concerns.

Keep the candidate, input, relevant conditions, observed result, and interpretation connected. Formatting, compilation, exercised behavior, and external effects establish different properties. State the conclusion at the scope the observation supports.

Use evidence produced by the task's assigned verification roles. Completion rests on the actual observations and artifacts in that evidence. Reuse valid observations for unchanged behavior with their producer, candidate, and conditions identified.

A failure can originate in the artifact or the observation method. Investigate the actual discrepancy before changing either. A repeatable command or script is useful when it makes the relevant comparison cheaper or easier to inspect; see [Build the tool first](./build-the-tool-first.md).
